# 🌞 Prayer Time Shadow Simulation

An interactive educational web simulation that visually demonstrates how Islamic prayer times were traditionally determined using the position of the sun ☀️ and the length of shadows 🌤️ before the invention of modern clocks.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

This project is a visual learning tool that explains how ancient people observed natural elements like:

- ☀️ Sun movement across the sky  
- 🌤️ Changing shadow length throughout the day  
- 🌅 Sunrise and sunset transitions  
- 🕌 Key Islamic prayer time markers  

Instead of relying on watches or digital clocks, people used **nature-based observation techniques** to estimate time accurately.

This simulation helps users understand those concepts in an interactive and visual way.

---

## 🎯 Purpose of the Project

The main goal of this project is to:

- Make prayer time calculation easier to understand visually  
- Demonstrate how shadow-based time tracking works  
- Provide an educational simulation for students and learners  
- Show how natural observation was used in early timekeeping systems

## 🧠 Concept Explanation

### 🌞 Zawal
Zawal is the time when the sun reaches its highest point in the sky, and the shadow becomes the shortest. After this point, the shadow starts increasing again, marking the beginning of Zuhr time.

### 🌤️ Sawa Naiza
Sawa Naiza refers to the time shortly after sunrise when the sun rises to a height approximately equal to a spear (naiza). At this stage, the sun is still low, and shadows remain long.

### 🕌 Traditional Method
In ancient times, people used:
- Stick-based shadow observation (Sundial method)
- Sun direction tracking
- Natural brightness of the sky

to estimate time without modern tools.
